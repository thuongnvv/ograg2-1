Q1: What is Creative Commons and what do you do?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
Creative Commons is the global nonprofit **Organization** that provides free legal tools (licenses, CC0, Public Domain Mark) to enable sharing and reuse of creative works【3】.  The central entity behind these tools is **INST:CreativeCommonsOrg**, whose properties describe it as “Creative Commons” with the purpose of offering those tools【1】.

What Creative Commons does:

* Publishes and maintains the six core **CCLicense** family of licenses (e.g., BY, BY‑NC, BY‑SA, etc.)【4】.  
* Makes the licenses and related tools publicly available via its website (https://creativecommons.org)【1】.  
* Does **not** give legal advice, track licensed material, or provide referral services (NegativeFact_NoLegalAdvice【10】, NegativeFact_NoTracking【13】, NegativeFact_NoReferral implied).  

Thus, Creative Commons creates and distributes standardized licensing tools, but it does not act as a law firm, a tracking database, or a referral service.

Q9: Why does Creative Commons run an annual fundraising campaign? What is the money used for and where does it go?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSWER:
Creative Commons runs an annual fundraising campaign to obtain **donations** that support its nonprofit **mission** of providing free legal tools (licenses, CC0, Public Domain Mark) that enable sharing and reuse of creative works【1†L1-L3】【3†L1-L3】.  

When a person performs a **DonateAction** (e.g., INST:Action_Donate) the money is given to the central organization **Creative Commons Org** (INST:CreativeCommonsOrg)【2†L1-L4】【5†L1-L4】. Because Creative Commons is a nonprofit that **does not charge fees**【4†L1-L2】 and does not collect data【8†L1-L2】, the donated funds are used to sustain its operations, develop and maintain its legal tools, and carry out its outreach and educational activities.  

The provided terms do not specify more detailed line‑item allocations; they only indicate that the money goes to the Creative Commons organization to support its overall mission.
